# Product

## Platform

web

## Users

Hobbyists who own a multi-colour 3D printer (Bambu Lab AMS or similar, sliced in OrcaSlicer or
Bambu Studio). They use LayerLift at home, at the desk beside the printer, to make things for
themselves: logo reliefs, signs and gifts, and Gridfinity organisers for their own drawers. They
know their printer and slicer well, including filament slots, layer heights and bed size.

## Product Purpose

LayerLift is a set of tools that go from an idea to a multi-colour print file with every part
already assigned to its AMS slot:

- **Image to Layers** turns flat-colour artwork into a stacked, multi-colour relief.
- **Gridfinity** plans a drawer and designs its baseplate and bins, with multi-colour labels.

Success means the downloaded 3MF opens in OrcaSlicer ready to slice, with colours on the right
slots and parts that fit, without the user fixing anything by hand.

## Positioning

Free and open source, with no project caps (the paid Gridfinity generators limit saved projects).
It is built around multi-colour AMS printing: parts are split per filament and pre-assigned to
slots, which the generic generators don't do. Gridfinity geometry is built in the browser, so the
preview updates live and there is no build queue.

## Operating Context

- The user has the printer, the AMS slot list and the slicer open alongside.
- For Gridfinity they measure a real drawer with a ruler or calipers and enter the numbers.
- Output goes to OrcaSlicer / Bambu Studio as 3MF (STL optional).
- Sessions are desktop-first; phones are used to check or tweak.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Image to Layers: colour analysis, filament mapping, per-colour and per-area heights, three
  stacking strategies, a 3D preview with drag-to-set heights, and a server-side build queue.
- Gridfinity: drawer grid and baseplate splitting for the bed, bins (dividers, scoops, label tabs,
  magnet/screw holes, lite base, hexagon walls), text and icon labels in their own filament.
- My printers: each printer's bed and loaded filaments; single-colour printers (no AMS) are supported.
- Optional accounts (PocketBase): saved builds, saved projects, printers, tiers, an admin page.
- Terminology to keep: filament, slot, AMS, layer, 3MF, bin, baseplate, cell, unit, lip, tab.

## Brand Commitments

- The name LayerLift and the three-layer "lifting" wordmark (gold, steel and ink layers,
  generated by `docs/brand/make_logo.py`) stay as the brand mark.
- Everything else visual (typefaces, palette, layout, component style) may be replaced.
- The user found the current interface generic: identical panels everywhere, a templated
  three-column layout, bland grey colour and type, and copy that explains too much.

## Evidence on Hand

- Sample artwork: `frontend/public/sample-space-turtles.png` (Space Turtles logo).
- No testimonials, user counts, press or pricing exist; don't invent any.

## Product Principles

1. The print file is the product: every screen serves getting a correct 3MF out.
2. Speak the printer's language: slots, layers, millimetres and cells, not abstractions.
3. Show the thing: the model and the drawer are the centre of every screen, and controls serve them.
4. No caps, no nagging: accounts add convenience, never unlock basics.
